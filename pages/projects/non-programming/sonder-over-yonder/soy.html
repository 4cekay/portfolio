Sonder Over Yonder
← Back

Wonder alone? Wander together?

A social decision-making board game for 3-5 players where everyday scenarios are solved by using individual traits and enlisting the help of other unique characters, building connections that ultimately determine each player’s ending.

  • Course: Foundations of Game Design (SFU)
  • Development Period: March - April, 2026
  • Deliverables: Complete board game and physical prototype
  • Roles: System Designer, Narrative Designer & Writer, Presentation Leader & Video Editor
  • Tools: Figma, Game Design Documents, After Effects
  • Team Members: Jenny Nguyen, Victoria Lee, Kristyn Tsui, Tawshif Al Mehrab

Key Takeaways

  • Design Objective:

    Design and develop a transformative tabletop game, where the goal is to evoke meaningful reflection and/or cultivate shifts in perspective.

    Through adding another layer to the purpose of gaming–which personal or social enjoyment is typically attributed to–that makes room for change and growth in mindset, this project aimed to shed light on the potential of play as a medium of social development.

    Developing a Solution:

    My team took very conscientious approaches during the ideation stage, aware of the careful balance required of a game effective in this particular genre–players must remain engaged within meaningful themes they are meant to interact with.

    During brainstorming, I found my team stuck on one big question:

    • What theme can be effectively communicated through a physical game, with limited world-building and player engagement relying primarily on mechanics and social atmosphere?

    Free-for-all brainstorming on a FigJam board: concepts, premises, meaningful areas of interest or exploration interests, etc.

    I then grouped together similar ideas in hopes of finding overlaps that could strike inspiration.

    The Six Degrees of Separation:

    “A theory suggesting that any two people on Earth are connected by, at most, six social connections or intermediaries.”

    Koenig-Workman, I. (2025). Six Degrees of Separation. The Decision Lab

  • Finalizing a Concept

    The "Six Degrees of Separation" idea proposed a more "wholesome" approach that explored human connectedness, an important yet often overlooked aspect in the grand schemes of every day. Switching gears to this new thematic potential kindled a discussion on interactions between strangers, protagonists and side characters, and the intricate web of stories comprising one’s surroundings.

    This led to the discovery of the term that built the board game’s premise:


    Sonder:

    “The realization and understanding that all other people have lives as complex as one’s own.”

    John Koenig, author of “The Dictionary of Obscure Sorrows”, who coined the term.

    Proposed Solution

    A simulation-based board game centered around different individuals living their own stories and experiences. Players will progress through the game’s narrative, facing opportunities to create connections and gain cumulative points as they encounter one another in overlapping scenarios.

    Earliest concept draft during the ideation stage, as a final candidate out of two.

  • Initial Mechanics Draft

    Choosing to explore themes of extrospection, connection, and togetherness addressed the concerns of:

    • A board game with a theme so heavy that engagement with the gameplay itself suffers

    • A board game with a compelling theme, but would be lacking in a physical medium (i.e. better suited through a digital environment with more room)

    The next step was helping my team past the hurdle of visualizing real gameplay for such a wide-scoped theme, which I sought to do by laying out hypothetical components with their thematic relations.

    Final Design Solution

    A choice-based roleplay/simulation board game where players take on characters with unique careers and traits, striving to complete their storyline. A turn-based narrative and gameplay structure of solving daily encounters will encourage interpersonal interactions through point systems and both rewards and penalties affecting progression.

    My solo brainstorm “concept web” on FigJam, which loosely laid out the mechanical potential of the premise to both clarify with examples and instill confidence within my team regarding our concept’s technical execution.

  • Character System - Careers & Traits

    Since outcomes depended heavily on player career and traits, the lineups went through several rounds of drafting, weighing options based on the following:

    • (Careers) Narrative scope and character arc potential, while being easy to research and fictionalize

    • (Careers) Familiar to general audiences to keep the game approachable

    • (Traits) Diverse range for unpredictable combinations to increase replayability through variability of character dynamics

    Initial ideation considered intersecting narratives (e.g. Lawyer and Police storylines being connected) which influenced selection at the time, even though it was omitted in the final design in order to simplify a long writing process, and put more emphasis on everyday connections.

    Character Story Progression

    Meanwhile, the earliest individual storyline drafts implemented a progression system that depended on accumulating points, forming “connections,” and branching outcomes based on trait combinations. This was later streamlined to reduce cognitive load, reducing the number of systems players needed to track and making each checkpoint an unlockable narrative segment only.

    Initial System & Theme Alignments

    Selected careers and traits were aligned to the core structure using the Mechanics → Dynamics → Aesthetics (MDA) Framework, mapping out how each works in cohesion:

    1. Mechanics (components, rules, actions) should lead to certain social dynamics
    2. Dynamics (e.g. banter and laughter, negotiation, cooperation) support desired aesthetic outcomes
    3. Aesthetics(e.g. meaningful interpersonal engagement, extrospective reflection, empathy and camaraderie) are intended facets of the player experience

    Although core gameplay revolves around navigating encounters, the impact is meant to come from the conscious choice to acknowledge, help, or connect with others rather than letting them pass by. This reinforces the game’s thematic goal of noticing and valuing the people around you.
